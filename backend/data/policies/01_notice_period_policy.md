# Notice Period Policy

**Version:** 2.1
**Effective From:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Human Resources

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the notice period obligations for employees resigning from the organisation, including how notice is served, what happens when notice is shortened or waived, and the treatment of garden leave.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all permanent employees. Contract and probationary employees are governed by their individual offer letters.

---

## 3. Standard Notice Periods by Grade

| Grade / Level | Notice Period |
|---|---|
| Junior (Grade 1–3) | 30 days |
| Mid-level (Grade 4–6) | 60 days |
| Senior / Lead (Grade 7–9) | 90 days |
| Manager and above | 90 days |
| Director and above | 90 days (negotiable with HR) |

The applicable grade is the employee's grade on the date the resignation letter is submitted.

---

## 4. Starting the Notice Period

The notice period begins on the calendar day immediately following the date the written resignation is received and acknowledged by the employee's manager or HR. Verbal resignations are not valid — a written resignation letter or email is required.

HR will issue a formal acknowledgement within 2 business days of receiving the resignation. If the manager does not acknowledge within 2 days, HR may acknowledge on their behalf and the notice period still begins from the original submission date.

---

## 5. Last Working Day

The last working day is calculated by adding the notice period days to the resignation date. If the calculated date falls on a weekend or public holiday, the last working day is the preceding business day.

Example: Resignation submitted on 1 October, notice period 60 days → last working day is 30 November (adjusted if it falls on a holiday).

---

## 6. Notice Period Buyout (Early Release)

An employee may request early release by paying the company the salary equivalent for the unserved notice days (notice buyout). The request must be:

1. Made in writing to HR at least 15 days before the requested early-release date.
2. Approved by the immediate manager and the HR Business Partner.
3. Finance must confirm the buyout amount before HR issues the early-release letter.

Notice buyout amounts are calculated on the employee's last drawn Gross Salary (Basic + DA + HRA) divided by 26 working days, multiplied by unserved days.

The company reserves the right to reject a notice buyout request if critical ongoing work would be materially impacted.

---

## 7. Notice Waiver by the Company

The company may, at its sole discretion, waive the remaining notice period and relieve the employee earlier than the last working day, with or without pay for the waived period. This decision is made by the HR Head with approval from the Business Head. The employee has no right to demand a notice waiver.

---

## 8. Garden Leave

During the notice period, the company may place an employee on garden leave — requiring the employee to stay away from the office and abstain from working for any other employer. During garden leave:

- Full salary and benefits continue to be paid.
- The employee remains an employee of the company and is bound by all confidentiality, non-compete, and non-solicit obligations.
- The employee must remain reachable during business hours for knowledge transfer queries.
- The employee must not access company systems unless explicitly authorised for KT purposes.

Garden leave is served as part of the notice period, not in addition to it.

---

## 9. Absconding During Notice Period

An employee who stops attending work or reporting in without approval during the notice period is treated as having absconded. In this case:

- Salary is not paid for the absent period.
- The company may recover the salary equivalent of the unserved days from full-and-final settlement.
- A formal termination letter is issued, which may affect rehire eligibility.
- Legal action may be initiated if the employment contract contained specific penalties for absconding.

---

## 10. Handover and Knowledge Transfer Obligations

Serving the notice period is not enough — the employee must actively participate in knowledge transfer (KT). This includes:

- Completing the KT document for all assigned projects and responsibilities.
- Making themselves available for transition calls and walkthroughs.
- Handing over access credentials, documentation, and ongoing work to the nominated successor.

If KT is not completed satisfactorily by the last working day, HR and the manager may delay issuing the relieving letter until KT is complete, subject to a maximum of 7 additional days.

---

## 11. Annual Leave During Notice Period

Accrued but unused annual leave may not normally be taken during the notice period. Any leave days taken during the notice period with manager approval will be deducted from accrued leave balance. Leave days taken will not extend the last working day.

---

## 12. Exceptions and Disputes

Any exception to this policy (e.g., immediate release for personal emergencies, extended notice for critical projects) must be approved in writing by the HR Head. Disputes about the notice period are escalated first to the HR Business Partner and then to the HR Head, whose decision is final.
