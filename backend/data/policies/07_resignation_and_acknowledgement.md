# Resignation and Manager Acknowledgement Policy
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective From:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Human Resources

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy covers the first two stages of the offboarding workflow: submitting a resignation (`RESIGNATION_SUBMITTED`) and the manager's acknowledgement of it (`MANAGER_ACK`). It explains what a valid resignation looks like, what the manager must do, and what happens if either step stalls.

---

## 2. Scope

All permanent and fixed-term employees who choose to leave the organisation voluntarily. Retirement and end-of-contract exits follow the same workflow but are initiated by HR rather than the employee.

---

## 3. What Makes a Resignation Valid

A resignation is valid only when it is **in writing**. Verbal notice, a message in a chat tool, or a comment in a one-to-one does not start the notice period.

A valid resignation must state:

1. The employee's intention to resign, unambiguously
2. The date of submission
3. The proposed last working day, or the intended notice period

Submitting the resignation through this platform satisfies the written requirement. An email to the reporting manager with HR copied is also accepted.

---

## 4. Submitting Through the Platform

The resignation form captures the exit type, the resignation date, and the intended last working day. On submission the platform:

- Creates the exit case and sets its stage to `RESIGNATION_SUBMITTED`
- Expands the full task checklist across all ten stages from the task templates
- Calculates due dates for every task by working backwards from the last working day
- Notifies the reporting manager and the HR Business Partner

One open exit case is permitted per employee. An employee with an active case cannot submit a second resignation; the existing case must be cancelled by HR first.

---

## 5. Manager Acknowledgement

The reporting manager must acknowledge the resignation within **2 working days** of submission.

Acknowledgement is an administrative confirmation that the manager has seen the resignation and accepts the stated last working day. It is not approval — an employee does not need permission to resign.

At acknowledgement the manager:

1. Confirms or disputes the proposed last working day against the notice period in the offer letter
2. Identifies the critical systems, processes, and relationships that require handover
3. Nominates a successor for knowledge transfer, or flags that one is not yet identified

The stage then advances to `KT_PLANNING`.

---

## 6. If the Manager Does Not Acknowledge

If the acknowledgement SLA is breached, the platform flags the task as overdue and it appears in the HR "Overdue" view. HR may acknowledge on the manager's behalf using the stage override, which requires a written reason and is recorded in the audit trail.

A manager's failure to acknowledge does not extend the notice period or delay the last working day. The clock runs from the date the written resignation was received, not from the date it was acknowledged.

---

## 7. Disputing the Last Working Day

Where the manager believes the proposed last working day does not honour the contractual notice period, the dispute is raised at acknowledgement and resolved by HR. HR's determination is based on the offer letter first and the Notice Period Policy second, and is final.

Pending resolution, the workflow continues against the originally proposed date so that clearance work is not delayed.

---

## 8. Withdrawing a Resignation

A resignation may be withdrawn only with the written agreement of both the reporting manager and HR, and only before the last working day. There is no automatic right to withdraw.

Withdrawal is more likely to be accepted where the role has not yet been posted externally and no offer has been extended to a replacement. If withdrawal is agreed, HR cancels the exit case; cancelled cases are retained, never deleted, so the history stays intact.

---

## 9. Confidentiality of a Resignation

Until the manager acknowledges and an internal communication plan is agreed, a resignation is treated as confidential between the employee, the manager, and HR. Teams are informed through an agreed announcement rather than informally.

---

## 10. Exit Type

The exit type recorded at resignation determines parts of the downstream workflow:

| Exit type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Resignation | Standard voluntary exit; full notice period applies |
| Retirement | Initiated by HR; gratuity and pension components apply |
| End of contract | Fixed-term expiry; no notice buyout applicable |
| Termination | Initiated by HR; separate process, not covered here |

---

## 11. Questions

Questions about whether a resignation has been received, or about acknowledgement delays, go to your HR Business Partner. Questions about the length of your notice period are answered in the Notice Period Policy.
