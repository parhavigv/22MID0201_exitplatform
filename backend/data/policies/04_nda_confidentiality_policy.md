# NDA and Confidentiality Policy — Exiting Employees

**Version:** 2.0
**Effective From:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Legal / Human Resources

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy describes the confidentiality obligations that continue after employment ends, the NDA acknowledgement that exiting employees must sign, and the consequences of breach.

---

## 2. Scope

All permanent employees, contractors, and interns, regardless of role or department. Confidentiality obligations survive the end of employment.

---

## 3. What Constitutes Confidential Information

Confidential information includes, but is not limited to:

- **Client information** — client names, contracts, pricing, project details, contact information, and any non-public information about a client's business shared during the engagement.
- **Business strategy** — product roadmaps, pricing models, go-to-market plans, M&A activity, partnership discussions.
- **Technology and IP** — source code, algorithms, system architecture, trade secrets, proprietary tools, and unpublished inventions.
- **Employee data** — compensation details, performance records, personal data of colleagues.
- **Financial data** — internal financial results, forecasts, budgets, and investor information not publicly disclosed.
- **Sales and pipeline** — prospect lists, deal values, win/loss data.

Information that becomes publicly available through no fault of the employee, or that the employee can demonstrate was already known to them independently, is not covered.

---

## 4. Obligations During the Notice Period

During the notice period the employee must:

1. Continue to protect all confidential information exactly as during active employment.
2. Not download, copy, or transmit company data to personal devices, personal email accounts, or cloud storage not authorised by the company.
3. Return or delete any company data on personal devices by the last working day.
4. Cooperate fully with any IT audit of devices and accounts.

---

## 5. Post-Employment Obligations

After the last working day, the employee must:

1. **Not disclose** any confidential information to any person or entity, including future employers.
2. **Not use** confidential information for any commercial purpose or to benefit any competitor.
3. **Delete or destroy** any confidential information in their possession (physical or digital) within 7 days of the last working day.
4. **Notify the company immediately** if they become aware that confidential information has been or may be disclosed by a third party.

The post-employment confidentiality obligation has **no time limit** for trade secrets. For other categories of confidential information, the obligation continues for **2 years** from the last working day unless the employee's contract specifies a longer period.

---

## 6. NDA Acknowledgement at Exit

As part of the exit process, every departing employee must sign and submit an NDA Acknowledgement Form confirming that:

1. They have returned all confidential information in their possession.
2. They understand their ongoing obligations as described in this policy and in their employment contract.
3. They have not, and will not, take any confidential information to their next employer or for any other purpose.
4. They have disclosed to the company any potential conflicts of interest with their next employer.

The signed NDA Acknowledgement is uploaded to the exit system. IT Clearance and the F&F settlement are not released until this acknowledgement is received.

---

## 7. Non-Compete and Non-Solicitation

Depending on the employee's role, their employment contract may also include:

- **Non-compete clause** — restricts joining a direct competitor for a defined period (typically 6–12 months) and in a defined geography. The enforceability of non-compete clauses varies by jurisdiction.
- **Non-solicitation clause** — restricts soliciting the company's clients, prospects, or employees for a defined period (typically 12 months).

Employees should review their individual contracts to confirm which clauses apply. HR can provide a copy of the relevant clauses on request.

---

## 8. Data Security During the Notice Period

During the notice period, IT will monitor for unusual data movement such as:

- Large volume downloads to external drives.
- Mass email forwarding to personal accounts.
- Bulk file transfers to personal cloud storage.

Any such activity may be investigated by the Security team. Employees found to be exfiltrating data will be subject to immediate termination, forfeiture of F&F, and legal action.

---

## 9. Consequences of Breach

A breach of confidentiality obligations may result in:

- Civil legal action for damages.
- Injunctive relief to prevent further disclosure.
- Reporting to relevant regulatory bodies if the breach involves regulated data (personal data under applicable privacy law, financial data, etc.).
- Clawback of severance or any settlement amounts already paid.

The company will take appropriate legal action commensurate with the severity of the breach.

---

## 10. Questions

Employees with questions about their specific obligations should contact HR or Legal before their last working day. Seeking clarity is always better than inadvertently violating the policy.
