# Exit Process Overview
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective From:** 2025-01-01
**Owner:** Human Resources

---

## 1. Purpose

This document is the map of the whole offboarding journey. It describes all ten stages in order, who owns each one, what has to happen before a stage can close, and where to look for the detailed rules. Use it to understand the shape of the process; use the individual policies for the specifics.

---

## 2. Scope

All employees leaving the organisation. Every exit runs through the same ten stages in the same order, regardless of department, grade, or exit type.

---

## 3. The Ten Stages

| # | Stage | Owner | What closes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Resignation Submitted | Employee | A valid written resignation is recorded |
| 2 | Manager Acknowledgement | Manager | Manager confirms receipt and the last working day |
| 3 | KT Planning | Manager | Topics listed and a successor nominated for each |
| 4 | KT Execution | Employee | Every topic verified or recorded as a gap |
| 5 | IT Clearance | IT | Devices returned, access revoked, certificate issued |
| 6 | Finance Clearance | Finance | Advances settled, claims submitted, no-dues issued |
| 7 | Admin and Asset Return | Admin | ID and access cards returned, desk and locker cleared |
| 8 | Exit Interview | HR | Interview completed, or declined and waived |
| 9 | Final Settlement | Finance | F&F calculated, approved, and paid |
| 10 | Relieving Letter | HR | Relieving and experience letters issued |

Stages run broadly in sequence, though clearance stages 5 to 7 often overlap in practice.

---

## 4. What Gates a Stage

A stage cannot be signed off while either of the following is true:

- Any task inside it is still open
- Any document uploaded against it is still awaiting review

This is enforced by the platform. A stage approval attempted while work is outstanding is rejected with the reason.

---

## 5. HR Stage Override

HR can force a stage forward when a gate cannot reasonably be met — an unreachable approver, an asset lost in transit, a task that no longer applies.

An override requires a written reason, is restricted to HR, and is written to the audit trail as its own action. It is the deliberate exception, not a shortcut for routine delays.

---

## 6. Task Ownership

Every task carries an owner role. Tasks owned by roles other than yours appear under "Waiting on others" and are not yours to complete.

| Owner | Typical tasks |
|---|---|
| Employee | Upload KT document, return devices, submit claims, complete interview |
| Manager | Acknowledge resignation, nominate successor, verify KT, sign off stages |
| IT | Revoke system access, collect hardware, issue IT clearance |
| Finance | Settle advances, issue no-dues, calculate and release F&F |
| HR | Schedule interview, issue relieving and experience letters |

Only HR can waive a task.

---

## 7. Due Dates and SLA Breaches

Due dates are calculated backwards from the last working day when the case is created. A task past its due date and not yet complete is marked SLA-breached and appears in the HR "Overdue" view and the manager's "Needs you" inbox.

An SLA breach is a signal, not a penalty. It does not change the last working day or the settlement timeline.

---

## 8. Finance Clearance

Finance clearance runs alongside IT and Admin clearance and must complete before final settlement is calculated. It covers:

1. **Salary advances** — outstanding balance recovered from the F&F
2. **Travel and expense claims** — all pending claims submitted before the cut-off; claims submitted after the cut-off may not be reimbursed
3. **Corporate card** — balance cleared and the card surrendered
4. **Loans** — any company loan settled or a repayment schedule agreed in writing
5. **No-dues certificate** — issued once the above are clear

A pending reimbursement claim is the most common cause of a delayed settlement. Submit claims early in the notice period rather than in the final week.

---

## 9. Relieving Letter and Experience Letter

The relieving letter is the final artefact of the process. It confirms that the employee has been formally released and that all clearances are complete.

| Item | Standard |
|---|---|
| Issued by | Human Resources |
| Issued after | All clearances complete and F&F settled |
| Timeline | Within 5 working days of settlement |
| Relieving letter states | Dates of employment, last working day, confirmation of clearance |
| Experience letter states | Dates of employment, final designation, and department |

Neither letter includes performance commentary. An employee who left without serving notice, or with assets unreturned, may have the relieving letter withheld until the matter is resolved.

## 10. Who to Contact

| Topic | Contact |
|---|---|
| Notice period, last working day, buyout | HR Business Partner |

## 11. Where the Detail Lives

| Question | Policy |

---

## 12. If Something Is Not Covered

Where a question is not addressed by any policy above, HR will answer it directly rather than the platform inferring an answer. An uncovered question is a gap in the policy set, and HR will record it for the next revision.
